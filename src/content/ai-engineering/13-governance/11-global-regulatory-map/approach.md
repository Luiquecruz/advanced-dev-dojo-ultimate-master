1. Keep the law as data, not code: a dated table where each rule names its jurisdiction, its source instrument, its status and its first day. Reviewing the law then means reviewing a table.
2. Describe the product in the same vocabulary as the rules: markets, and per feature what it does, which data it uses and who it affects. A shared tag vocabulary is what makes matching mechanical.
3. Match markets with hierarchy in mind: a federal rule reaches every state market, a state rule reaches only its own.
4. Match conditions as "every constraint the rule sets is met by at least one value", so a rule can be as broad (everyone) or as narrow (credit decisions on personal data of consumers) as the law.
5. Evaluate status on a date, not "now": the same map answers what applies today, what applies at launch, and what a bill would add if passed.
6. Keep the source on every obligation and the review date on the table, and flag the table when it is older than a quarter, so stale law is visible instead of silently trusted.
