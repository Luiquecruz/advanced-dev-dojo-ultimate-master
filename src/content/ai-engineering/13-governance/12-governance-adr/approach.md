1. Read the briefing as findings, policies and facts. Every finding becomes a control, every policy a number the plan must respect, every fact (markets, features, dates) an input to the regulatory map.
2. Lay out the nine layers and give each one an owner who can actually act, keeping audit independent from the teams it audits.
3. Place each control at the earliest enforcement point that can check it: runtime for calls, CI for changes, process only for what code cannot judge, always with evidence.
4. Fix the sprawl first: owners, expiring credentials, identity per agent, deny-by-default decisions and a tamper-evident log. Every later control depends on knowing who did what.
5. Gate data by purpose and models by evals, with retention and migration dates that come from the briefing rather than from defaults.
6. Size budgets from the monthly limit down to features, with alerts before the hard limit and rate limits for loops.
7. Keep humans meaningful where decisions affect people: a sustainable review volume, measured overrides and an appeal path.
8. Map obligations per feature and market with statuses on today's date, keep bills as watch items, and schedule the quarterly review. Write the ADR last, citing the facts that rule out each alternative.
