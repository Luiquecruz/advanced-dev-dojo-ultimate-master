Start with the numbers. The types at the horizon are today's types plus the quarterly growth over the horizon. The peak rate is per minute and for the whole fleet: convert it to seconds and divide by the instances. The linear worst case is the cost of failing every case of one statement that holds all the types you will have then.
---
Turn the CPU share into a budget per message: 2% of one core is 0.02 CPU-seconds every second, shared by the dispatches of that second. Compare the linear worst case with it, today and at the horizon. Then look at the payloads: what does `type(payload)` return for every webhook message, and what happens to a method you add to a generated class?
---
Each mechanism comes with its own way to add a type: a registry is filled by registration, a `match` is extended by editing it, polymorphism by subclassing. Count how often each one touches the shared file per quarter, and remember that a default handler answers messages that should be dead-lettered.
