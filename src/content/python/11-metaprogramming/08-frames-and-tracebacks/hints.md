Count frames, not lines of text. `inspect.currentframe()` is the frame of `caller_name` itself, so its `f_back` is the function that called `caller_name` and the next `f_back` is that function's caller. Walk `skip + 2` steps, stop as soon as a step returns `None`, and read `f_code.co_name`. Parsing `traceback.format_stack()` with a regular expression is the `new Error().stack` habit, and it fails on names such as `<lambda>`.
---
`traceback.extract_stack(frame, limit=n)` starts at the frame you pass and returns the `n` most recent entries, oldest first. Pass the caller's frame (`sys._getframe(1)`), otherwise `call_path` ends its own list. For `raised_in`, `traceback.extract_tb(error.__traceback__)` is ordered from the catching frame to the raising frame: you want the last entry, and an empty summary means the error was never raised.
---
Build `traceback.TracebackException.from_exception(error)` once and walk its `__cause__` and `__context__` attributes, which are `TracebackException` objects too. Take `__cause__` first, stop when `__suppress_context__` is true, and append `exc_type_str` at each step. It already stops at an exception it has seen, so a looping chain ends, and it holds no frames. Reverse the list at the end.
