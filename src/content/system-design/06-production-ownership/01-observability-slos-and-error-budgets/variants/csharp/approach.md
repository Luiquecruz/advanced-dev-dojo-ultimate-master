1. Start from the definition of an SLI: a ratio of good events to all events. A ratio needs a rule for an empty window, and the useful one is that no requests means nothing failed, so availability is `1` and the error rate `0`.
2. Read the error budget as the SLO turned around: `1 - slo` is the share of events allowed to fail, and multiplying it by the traffic of the period turns that share into a number of events. Keep it unrounded, so the same formula works for a million requests and for a quiet service.
3. Derive everything else in the budget from that one number: what remains (negative once overspent) and the share consumed, deciding the division by zero explicitly instead of letting `double.NaN` leak into a dashboard.
4. A burn rate compares two rates, not two counts: the error rate observed in a window against the error rate the SLO allows. That ratio is why the same number of errors means an outage at low traffic and nothing at high traffic.
5. The page condition is a conjunction. The long window says a significant share of the budget is gone, the short window says the burn has not stopped; with one window alone, or with the two joined by `||`, the alert fires on short bursts or keeps firing long after the fix.
